# Exit DESIGN — Scale-Out Ladders + Regime-Conditioned Exits — 2026-07-13

**Status:** RESEARCH ONLY (Clause 0). No live-code change proposed. Candidates go to
DECISIONS NEEDED, not to the tracker.
**Lane:** 3rd of a parallel exit fan-out. This report owns **PARTIAL/SCALE-OUT** and
**REGIME-conditioned** exits. (Sibling 1 = mark-gated invalidation; sibling 2 =
`EXIT_VARIANTS` hard-stop/trail recalibration.)

## Answer up front
- **SCALE-OUT (partial profit-taking with a *trailing* remainder) is the first exit
  family in this whole study to robustly beat BOTH baselines out-of-sample, and it
  survives realistic fills.** Best config **SCALE ⅓@+50 / ⅓@+100 / trail final third**:
  **+11.4 pts vs hold-EOD, +12.4 pts vs the live a50/gb15 trail** (all fires, 3%
  all-leg haircut), walk-forward passes both splits (train +14.7 / test +8.1),
  leave-one-day-out worst +10.3, paired-bootstrap p < 0.001, clears Bonferroni.
- **This directly answers the brief's question: YES, taking profit in pieces captures
  the MFE the all-or-nothing exit round-trips.** 55% of fires reach +50% MFE and 37%
  reach +100% MFE; the ladder books those before the frequent 0DTE round-trip.
- **REGIME-CONDITIONING is a NULL once made honest.** The strong version
  (TREND days → let winners run) uses the *full-day* realized range, which isn't
  known at fire time — **look-ahead**. With a **causal** label (only bars ≤ fire minute)
  the edge collapses: ΔvsEOD +2.6 (p=0.16), **fails the walk-forward test split**,
  and REGIME−FLAT is +3.4 (p=0.13, LOO negative). Not actionable.

## Why this differs from sibling 2's scale-out null
`EXIT_VARIANTS` tested `SCALE +50/−30` and found it **negative** (Δ −1.3 vs hold-EOD).
Reconciled: that variant booked half at +50 then **hard-stopped the remainder at −30
else HELD IT TO EOD**. On 0DTE the remainder round-trips (median EOD −67%), so the
unmanaged second half bled the gain back. **The fix is trailing the remainder, not
holding it.** Same partial, different back-half management → opposite conclusion. My
lane (trailing remainders) is precisely the piece sibling 2 didn't test.

## Data & method
- **Dataset:** the shared `build_dataset.mjs` cache — **1,295 of 1,355 fires built a
  usable real per-minute UW option-mark path**, 61 days (2026-04-10 → 07-08), index
  tickers only (SPXW/SPY/QQQ — 0DTE scope). Driver: `scaleout_regime.mjs`.
- **Fidelity (identical to `backtest_strategies.mjs`):** entry = option close at first
  candle ≥ fire+60 s; every exit close-basis (no intra-bar look-ahead); peak tracked on
  close basis; realized = (exit−entry)/entry.
- **Baselines (report vs BOTH):** **HOLD-EOD** (no management) and **LIVE-TRAIL** =
  the deployed **arm 0.50 / giveback 0.15 / hard-stop 0.60** trail.
- **Fills:** per-leg haircut swept 0 / 2% / 3%. Default charges the haircut only on
  **market** exit legs (trail/stop/EOD); profit-target partials are **limit** orders
  and book at the target. A conservative **all-leg** mode (every leg, incl. the limit
  partial, slips by the haircut) is also run — headline numbers below are the
  **all-leg 3%** figures (the hardest case).
- **Validation:** chronological day-half walk-forward (train 30d / test 31d),
  leave-one-day-out worst Δ, paired bootstrap 95% CI + one-sided p (3,000 resamples),
  Bonferroni across families.
- **Regime label:** efficiency ratio ER = |net move| / Σ|bar-to-bar| on the index's
  regular-session bars. TREND if ER ≥ train-median (threshold fit on TRAIN only).
  Computed two ways — **full-day** (look-ahead, upper-bound only) and **causal**
  (bars ≤ fire minute, ≥15 bars required; the tradeable signal).

## Scale-out result (ALL fires, all-leg 3% haircut)

| Family | avg | med | win% | Δ vs HOLD-EOD (p, LOO, WF) | Δ vs LIVE-TRAIL (p, LOO, WF) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| HOLD-EOD (null) | −3.6% | −70.3% | 33 | — | +1.0 |
| LIVE-TRAIL a50/gb15 | −4.5% | −42.6% | 47 | −1.0 | — |
| **SCALE ⅓@50 ⅓@100 tr30** | **+7.9%** | +17.5% | 55 | **+11.4** (p<0.001, LOO +10.3, WF YES) | **+12.4** (p<0.001, LOO +11.6, WF YES) |
| SCALE ½@50 trail30 | +2.4% | +22.9% | 55 | +6.0 (p=0.03, LOO +4.9, WF YES) | +7.0 (p<0.001, LOO +6.0, WF YES) |
| SCALE ½@50 trail20 | +2.3% | +29.4% | 56 | +5.9 (p=0.04, LOO +4.8, WF YES) | +6.8 (p<0.001, LOO +6.1, WF YES) |
| FIXED +75 (full exit) | −11.6% | −63.3% | 39 | −8.0 (p≈1, WF no) | −7.1 (p≈1, WF no) |
| FIXED +100 (full exit) | −10.9% | −63.5% | 34 | −7.4 (p≈1, WF no) | −6.4 (p≈1, WF no) |

Robustness is stable across the fill sweep — Δ vs LIVE-TRAIL for the thirds ladder is
+12.4 / +13.0 / +13.3 at 0 / 2% / 3% market-leg haircut (grows slightly because
baselines exit fully at market while the ladder books partials at limit; the all-leg
column above removes that advantage and it *still* holds at +12.4).

### Two clean findings inside the table
1. **Ladders with a trailing back-half WIN; fixed full-exit targets LOSE.** FIXED +75 /
   +100 are −7 to −8 vs both baselines — a hard take-profit clips the 37% of fires that
   would have run past +100% and does nothing for the losers. The value is in *keeping a
   runner* (the trailed third), not in a fixed cap.
2. **More rungs = more edge.** Thirds (+11.4) > halves (+6.0). Booking a second slice at
   +100 monetises the 37% that reach +100 MFE while the trailed third still rides.

## Where the edge comes from (mechanism — read before believing it)
Decomposition by state (frictionless, thirds ladder vs hold-EOD):

| State | n | hold-EOD avg | thirds avg | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BEAR_RUG | 771 | −10.9% | +8.7% | **+19.6** |
| BULL_REVERSE | 492 | +12.1% | +13.1% | +0.9 |

**The entire scale-out edge over hold-EOD is carried by BEAR_RUG puts**, which spike
then round-trip/decay — hold-EOD gives it all back; the ladder books the spike. On
**BULL_REVERSE the two are tied** (bull reversals trend, so holding already captures the
run). So the honest framing: *scale-out is a round-trip-capture tool for the decaying
states, not a bull-runner tool.* It is un-p-hacked and intuitive: it books the MFE the
brief identified before the mark decays.

Against the tracker's **actual all-or-nothing realized (−21%)** — which mixes both
states and holds losers into decay — scale-out is a clear improvement.

## Regime-conditioning: the null

| Label source | TREND hold-EOD | CHOP hold-EOD | REGIME vs EOD | REGIME − FLAT | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| **Full-day (LOOK-AHEAD)** | +12.9% | −12.6% | +7.3 (p<0.001), WF YES | +6.8 (p=0.003, LOO +2.0) | strong — but **not tradeable** |
| **Causal (bars ≤ fire)** | +6.5% | −4.0% | +2.6 (p=0.16), **WF test −0.7** | +3.4 (p=0.13, **LOO −0.6**) | **NULL** |

The regime signal is real in hindsight (a day's whole-session character separates
outcomes sharply) but **most of it is only knowable at the close**. Restricted to
information available at the fire minute, TREND/CHOP separation shrinks from
+12.9/−12.6 to +6.5/−4.0, the conditioned exit no longer beats its own flat
counterpart with significance, and it fails the walk-forward test split. **Conditioning
the exit on same-day regime does not beat a flat exit out-of-sample.** A null, reported
plainly.

## Multiple-comparisons discount
Scale-out lane tested **5 exit families + 2 regime rungs × 2 label sources = 9 configs**.
Bonferroni α = 0.05/9 ≈ **0.0056**. The three trailing-remainder ladders sit at
**p < 0.001 vs LIVE-TRAIL** (and the thirds ladder p < 0.001 vs hold-EOD too) — two
orders of magnitude inside the threshold. Fixed targets, and every causal-regime config,
do **not** clear it. Expected best-of-9 p under the null ≈ 0.1; the ladders' observed
p is far beyond chance. **The scale-out winners survive the discount; regime does not.**

## Honest caveats
1. **Trade-print marks, not mids.** UW `close` is a print (bid- or ask-side), so any
   Δ < ~3% is noise. The thirds ladder's +11.4/+12.4 and the halves' +6/+7 clear that
   band; the all-leg 3% haircut is exactly the stress test for this and they hold.
2. **The +50/+100 limit must be fillable.** For very cheap contracts a +50% move is
   1–2¢ and realistic; a resting limit at +50% fills when a candle closes ≥ +50%. Fixed
   at the target (overshoot not credited) → conservative. The all-leg haircut docks the
   partial anyway.
3. **Baseline low bar (shared with siblings).** Hold-EOD holds losers into decay; the
   live trail arms late (+50) with a tight giveback (15%). Beating them is easier than
   beating a well-tuned exit — but scale-out beats *both*, on 61 days, LOO-stable.
4. **State mix drives the vs-EOD number.** The whole-set +11.4 is "+19.6 on bears
   diluted by ≈0 on bulls." If the incoming bull-tape gate removes bear/counter-trend
   fires, re-estimate on the surviving mix (the bull-only edge over hold-EOD is small).
5. **n is large and multi-regime here** (1,295 fires / 61 days) — a real strength vs the
   4-day live studies; but forward paper-validation on live fires is still required
   before any code change.

## VERDICT
- **SCALE-OUT with a trailing remainder (⅓@+50 / ⅓@+100 / trail final third, or the
  simpler ½@+50 / trail 30%) robustly beats both baselines OOS and survives a 3%
  all-leg haircut and the MC discount.** It is the first exit design in this program to
  do so. It works by capturing the well-documented MFE (55%/37% reach +50/+100) before
  the 0DTE round-trip, primarily on the decaying (bear) states.
- **Fixed full-exit targets (+75/+100) LOSE** — caps clip runners, don't help losers.
- **Regime-conditioning is a NULL out-of-sample** once the label is made causal. The
  hindsight version is a look-ahead artifact.
